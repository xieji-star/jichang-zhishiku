1. Introduction
                                                                                  Figure 1. Schematic Diagram of the Module Bending
Organic Light-emitting Diode (OLED) display devices,                                   Process. (a) Before bending. (b) After bending
characterized by their ultra-thin and lightweight design,
bendability, and high color gamut, are widely utilized in the field
of electronic displays. Foldable OLED screens have emerged as a
novel form of smart terminal displays, with the foldable display
market gradually expanding[1, 2]. The core component of foldable
mobile terminals is the flexible OLED display module, which is
composed of multiple layers stacked together to form a layered
structure. These layers are bonded using Optical Clear Adhesive
(OCA).

Within the OLED module, the support layer is a critical component
that determines the thickness, weight, bendability, and impact
resistance of the screen. Currently, support layers are typically
made from metallic materials such as stainless steel, titanium
alloys, and copper alloys[3]. To accommodate the bendability of
OLED modules, the bending area of the support layer is often
designed with a hollowed-out through-hole structure. By
modifying the structural dimensions of these holes, the deformation
consistency during the bending process can be adjusted. This study
focuses on the mechanical behavior of the metallic support layer in
flexible OLED modules during bending. Through finite element
simulation, the research explores flexible design solutions for the
metallic support layer structure, aiming to optimize the modulus
distribution in the bending region. The goal is to reduce local stress
concentrations and enhance the bending reliability of the OLED
module.
