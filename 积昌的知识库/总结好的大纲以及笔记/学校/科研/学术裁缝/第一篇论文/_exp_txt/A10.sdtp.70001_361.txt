                                                                      Historically, crease mitigation has heavily relied on improving
                                                                      the physicochemical properties of PSAs (e.g., stress relaxation or
                                                                      recovery rates). However, material development is inherently
                                                                      time-consuming and prone to reaching asymptotic
                                                                      physicochemical limits. Our findings regarding () propose a
                                                                      fundamental paradigm shift from a "material-dependent"
                                                                      approach to "geometric compensation." The analysis confirms
                                                                      that by optimizing structural symmetry, the shear load transferred
                                                                      to the PSA layers can be intrinsically reduced. This implies that
                                                                      structural balance can effectively compensate for material
                                                                      limitations. Consequently, this "Geometric Compensation"
                                                                      strategy empowers engineers to bypass material development
                                                                      bottlenecks and achieve superior mechanical reliability using
                                                                      currently available materials.
