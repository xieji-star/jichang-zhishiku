Flexible film are notoriously difficult to fully conform to non-
developable surface such as spheres [1-2]. The greatest challenge
when trying to attaching flexible display to 3D spherical glass is
avoiding wrinkling phenomenon at the edge of flexible display. As
shown schematically in figure1a, if we assume for a moment that
the dis-shaped flexible film with radius W is inextensible in the
radial direction and that the central point of the display is attached
to the north pole of the spherical surface with radius R, then each
circle with radius r is approximately mapped to the circle Sr on the
sphere at distance r from the north pole. The arc length of Sr then
can be calculated to be 2Rsin(r/R). Apparently the arc length of Sr
is less than 2r, therefore the edge of the film is subjected to
azimuthal compressive strain. Roughly, the geometric strain at edge
of the film is ~1/6(W/R)2. One must note that the preceding account
is oversimplified, flexible film is not inextensible and can slip along
the sphere. If the bending strain in the radial direction is considered,
the compressive stress only occurs at the region that radius bigger
than L as shown in Figure 1b.

A compressed elastic film on a compliant substrate can form
winkles when the compressive strain is large enough [3], a simple
example is the buckling of a sheet of paper when compressed along
two edges. Since the tensile stiffness is significant greater than the
bending stiffness for film structure, this instability occurs because
